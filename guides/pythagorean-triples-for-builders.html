Pythagorean Triples for Builders: Beyond 3-4-5

By the 000345 editorial team · Updated October 2026

A Pythagorean triple is a set of three whole numbers that satisfy a² + b² = c². Each one gives a right triangle with no fractions or decimals to measure — ideal when you are holding a tape in the wind. 3-4-5 is the famous one, but it is not always the best fit.

Useful triples

LegsDiagonalShapeWhen to use it
3 – 45Near squareGeneral layout
5 – 1213Long and narrowLong walls, fence corners, narrow side yards

7 – 2425Very longLong building lines where space to the side is tight
20 – 2129Almost squareSquare rooms and pads
9 – 4041Very longLong property-line work
12 – 3537LongLarge slabs with limited side clearance

Every multiple of a triple is also a triple: 10-24-26 is 5-12-13 doubled, 16-30-34 is 8-15-17 doubled.

Generating your own

For any two whole numbers m > n, the numbers m² − n², 2mn and m² + n² form a triple. With m = 2 and n = 1 you get 3, 4, 5. With m = 3 and n = 2 you get 5, 12, 13. With m = 4 and n = 1 you get 15, 8, 17.

Metric examples

In metric, scale by tenths: 0.5 / 1.2 / 1.3 m, 0.8 / 1.5 / 1.7 m, or 3 / 4 / 5 m. Because the triple is a ratio, the unit does not matter.

If none of the triples fits your space, use any two leg lengths and let the right triangle calculator give you the exact diagonal.

Questions

Is 6-8-10 a different triple from 3-4-5?

It is the same triangle scaled by two. Builders call multiples by their own numbers because those are the numbers on the tape.
