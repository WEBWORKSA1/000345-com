How Square Is Square Enough? Layout Tolerances Explained

By the 000345 editorial team · Updated October 2026

No layout is perfect. The question is how much error the next trade can absorb. A foundation that is 3/8" out may be fine for framing that can be adjusted on the sill plate; the same 3/8" in a run of kitchen cabinets is a visible gap at the countertop.

Angle error grows with distance

A corner that is off by a fraction of a degree looks fine up close, but the offset grows linearly with distance. The offset at the far end is roughly distance × tan(error).

Corner errorOffset at 10 ftOffset at 40 ft
0.1°about 3/16"about 13/16"
0.25°about 1/2"about 2-1/8"
0.5°about 1-1/16"about 4-3/16"
1°about 2-1/8"about 8-3/8"

Typical working tolerances

The figures below are common residential practice, not code. Your plans, your engineer and your local authority have the final word.

How to hit tighter tolerances

  • Use the longest triangle the site allows (15-20-25 instead of 3-4-5).
  • Hook the tape on nails, not pencil marks.
  • Keep tapes taut and off the ground.
  • Check both diagonals of every rectangle.
  • Re-measure after anything is moved or fastened.

Questions

Is a laser better than 3-4-5?

Lasers are faster on big sites, but a 3-4-5 check with a tape is the standard way to verify any instrument. Many crews use both.
