By the 000345 editorial team · Last reviewed October 2026 · How we calculate

Ordering too little concrete leaves a cold joint in the middle of your pour; ordering too much wastes money on material you cannot return. This calculator turns slab dimensions into an order: volume in cubic yards and cubic metres, the number of premix bags, and a ready-mix quantity rounded up to the quarter yard most plants deliver in.

Formula

Volume = length × width × thickness. In imperial units, convert thickness to feet (inches ÷ 12), multiply to get cubic feet, then divide by 27 for cubic yards. In metric, convert thickness to metres and the result is already in cubic metres.

Worked example

A 12 × 10 ft patio at 4" thick: 12 × 10 × (4 ÷ 12) = 40 ft³ ÷ 27 = 1.48 yd³. Add 10% and you need about 1.63 yd³ — order 1.75 yd³, or about 74 bags of 80 lb premix (each yields roughly 0.6 ft³).

Bags or ready-mix?

Under about 1 yd³, bagged concrete mixed on site is usually cheaper. Above 1.5–2 yd³, a ready-mix truck saves hours of mixing and gives a more consistent slab. Many plants have a short-load fee for small orders, so ask before you book.

Frequently asked questions

How many 80 lb bags make a cubic yard?

About 45. Each 80 lb bag yields roughly 0.6 cubic feet, and a cubic yard is 27 cubic feet.

How much extra concrete should I order?

5–10% covers spillage, an uneven base and forms that bow slightly. Use the higher end for hand-dug or uneven subgrade.

Is 4 inches thick enough for a driveway?

For cars it can work on a well-compacted base, but 5–6 inches is the common recommendation for driveways, especially with heavier vehicles.
