By the 000345 editorial team · Last reviewed October 2026 · How we calculate

Pitch tells you how steep a roof is, which decides the roofing material you can use, the length of every rafter, and how much surface you will be covering. Enter any rise and run — for example, hold a level 12" out from the roof and measure straight down to the surface — and the calculator converts it to every common format.

Pitch, angle and slope

  • Pitch is rise per 12 units of run: 6:12 means 6" of rise for every 12" horizontally.
  • Angle is arctan(rise ÷ run). 6:12 is 26.57°.
  • Slope as a percentage is rise ÷ run × 100. 6:12 is 50%.
  • Rafter multiplier is √(1 + (rise ÷ run)²). Multiply plan area or horizontal run by it to get the sloped length or area.

Worked example

A house footprint with overhangs measures 1,200 ft² and the pitch is 6:12. Roof area = 1,200 × 1.118 ≈ 1,342 ft², or 13.4 roofing squares (one square = 100 ft²). Add 10–15% for waste, hips and starter courses when ordering shingles.

Frequently asked questions

What is the most common roof pitch?

In North American houses, 4:12 to 9:12 covers most roofs, with 6:12 being very common.

What is the minimum pitch for asphalt shingles?

Most manufacturers require at least 2:12, with special underlayment between 2:12 and 4:12. Below that, use a low-slope membrane.

How do I measure pitch from inside the attic?

Hold a level horizontally against the underside of a rafter, mark 12" along the level, and measure vertically from that mark up to the rafter. That number is the rise per 12.
